Cost of tariffs

Today the Trump Tariffs cost you an average of

$2.52

Total cost to date: $177 billion collected in 2026 alone — and a broader $337 billion in net customs revenue from January 2025 through June 2026, per a separate independent analysis.

From the source

The Tax Policy Center "estimates that tariffs announced by the Trump administration will impose an average burden of roughly $920 per tax unit (household) during calendar year 2026," and notes the burden is regressive: households in the bottom income quintile face a 0.8 percentage-point increase in their average federal tax rate, compared with a 0.6 percentage-point increase for households in the top quintile.

— Tax Policy Center, Tracking the Trump Tariffs

Additional sources

  • Peterson Institute for International Economics: Tariff Revenue Tracker

    Reports $174 billion in net customs receipts for January–September 2025 and another $163 billion for October 2025–June 2026 — the source of the $337 billion combined figure above.

  • Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond: How Much Revenue Has Been Raised by Tariffs So Far?

    Notes $287 billion in total customs duties, taxes, and fees collected in calendar year 2025 — a 192% increase over 2024.

  • Yale Budget Lab: Tracking the Economic Effects of Tariffs

    An open-source, continuously updated model of tariff rates and their price-level and revenue effects.

Methodology. The per-household daily average divides the Tax Policy Center's 2026 household cost estimate ($920) by 365. The "total cost to date" figure blends two different but complementary measures: TPC's own 2026 collection estimate, and PIIE's cumulative net customs receipts across a longer window (January 2025–June 2026) — shown together because they answer slightly different questions (household burden vs. total federal revenue collected).
